# Inner Leadership — Brand Memory & Canonical Preferences

Version: 1.1  
Status: CURRENT

This file contains stable creative preferences learned from explicit approval/rejection. It is not a transcript dump.

## Core memory rule

Approved work teaches the system what to make next. Preserve the approved asset **and** capture why it was approved.

## Visual language

- Premium hand-drawn editorial realism for conceptual illustrations.
- Graphite pencil, charcoal, fine sepia ink, restrained watercolor/gouache.
- Visible hand marks, paper grain, slight smudges, organic imperfect edges.
- Warm parchment/beige/cream base.
- Walnut/dark brown, deep forest green, muted clay, charcoal, cream.
- Low saturation; serious, calm, intelligent, human and premium.
- Avoid cartoons, glossy stock style, clinical visuals, SaaS-template polish, bright children’s-brand colors.

## Jewish / Israeli context

- Visibly Jewish and Israeli where relevant, without costume or caricature.
- Natural kippot, modest contemporary Israeli clothing, mezuzah, believable Hebrew sefarim/siddurim, Jerusalem-stone/lime-plaster textures, warm wood and muted Israeli greenery when contextually natural.
- Broadly religious/traditional Jewish, not specifically Haredi or secular.
- Do not default to black hats, uniforms, caricatures or staged Shabbat tableaux.
- Jewish details should feel lived-in, not pasted on.

## People / age direction

- Canonical public age range: boys 7–13.
- Generated boys should visually read within that range unless a specific age is requested.
- Avoid making every boy look 12–14.
- The image does not have to literally depict the named curriculum activity. It may show a boy who embodies concentration, agency, judgment, responsibility, confidence, thoughtful communication or mastery.
- Avoid superhero confidence, adult-like posing and stock-model perfection.

## Real photos vs generated illustrations

- **Real photos** = credibility, actual program proof, founder, actual hands-on work.
- **Generated editorial illustrations** = conceptual outcomes, future curriculum scenes, ideal family/communication scenes.
- **Icons** = repeated conceptual navigation.
- Repeated sets must remain visually consistent: six outcome cards = one icon family; four curriculum modules = one illustration family/aspect ratio.
- Do not present generated children as documentary proof of actual participants.

## Current real-photo direction

- The real soldering/electronics photo is the current preferred hero because it communicates concentration, Jewish identity, hands-on mastery and credibility.
- Use the current real-program photo set in one dedicated proof/gallery section with consistent crops and spacing.
- Do not insert one real photo into only one curriculum-module card while the other modules use illustrations.
- Founder uses the real founder photo, not a generated identity.
- Preserve originals unchanged; derivatives/crops are separate.
- Public-use permission is confirmed for the current six-image real-media set as of 2026-08-21.
- Future real child images still require separate confirmation before public promotion.

## Copy / voice

- Professional, direct, concise, high-conviction and psychologically literate.
- Explain what the service is before selling abstract transformation.
- Current service framing: **“A 12-Week Hands-On Self-Governance Program for Boys Ages 7–13.”**
- Prefer mechanism + outcome: what the program does, how it works, what it develops.
- Avoid excessive poetry, sentimental family language, repetitive “he knows how to…” constructions, vague therapy-funnel copy and generic “transform your child” claims.
- Aspirational is fine when concrete and credible.

## Developmental themes

- Self-governance
- Critical thinking
- Communication
- Agency / internal ownership
- Responsibility
- Values, identity, purpose and goals
- Problem solving
- Emotional regulation in functional context
- Bodily awareness
- Confidence from real mastery
- Social navigation

## Landing-page visual preferences

- Hero may use real photography instead of forcing the sketch style everywhere.
- Separate real proof photography from generated conceptual illustration.
- Repeated module/outcome sets must be equal in visual class, scale and spacing.
- The site should feel like one coherent system, not a collage of individually good graphics.
- Use intentional responsive crops.

## Image-prompt preferences

- Every production prompt must be standalone with the complete art direction baked in.
- Include subject, age, Jewish context, Israeli setting, material/style, palette, composition, emotional objective and strict avoid-list.
- Do not require the user to prepend a master-style block.
- When an outcome can be shown indirectly, prefer a compelling human scene over literal diagramming.

## Current avoid list

- Generic therapy stock photos
- Sterile clinics
- Bright school/children graphics
- Cartoon/Pixar/Disney look
- Glossy corporate photography
- Fake high-fives/teamwork poses
- Overly poetic copy
- Random visual mixing inside repeated sets
- Boys who visually read older than the intended range unless intentional
- Fake readable Hebrew inside generated images
- Jewish stereotypes as visual shorthand

## Memory update standard

Add a preference only when:

1. the user explicitly approves/rejects it; or
2. the preference is strongly repeated across decisions.

Put it in the smallest relevant category. Do not duplicate it everywhere.

When a preference is superseded, preserve the history briefly rather than silently erasing it.
